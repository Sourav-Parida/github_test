RETAILMIND AI PROJECT DESCRIPTION

Project Summary
---------------
RetailMind AI is an end-to-end learning project that applies machine learning and deep learning to realistic retail use cases. The project is implemented through a series of 13 daily modules, with each stage building on the previous stage and saving trained models and measurable results for comparison.

The project covers four main business areas:
1. Predicting customer churn.
2. Classifying retail product images.
3. Forecasting store-item demand.
4. Analysing customer-review sentiment and routing review-derived support tickets.

Project Structure
-----------------
- data/: Source datasets and generated or downloaded training data.
- notebooks/: Jupyter notebooks for day-wise workflows and experiments.
- src/: Python modules containing data preparation, training, evaluation, forecasting, vision, and text-processing code.
- models/: Saved classical, TensorFlow, Keras, Transformer, and VAE models.
- reports/: Metrics, predictions, comparison tables, plots, attention weights, and explainability images.

Phase 1: Customer Churn Prediction - Days 1-5
---------------------------------------------
Objective:
Use Telco customer account and service information to predict whether a customer is likely to leave.

Topics completed:
- Data loading and preprocessing.
- Handling missing values.
- Encoding categorical fields and scaling numeric features.
- Logistic Regression baseline modelling.
- Comparison of Decision Trees, Random Forest, and RBF SVM models.
- Cross-validation and recall-focused model selection.
- Hyperparameter tuning.
- Feature engineering, including tenure groups and charge relationships.
- Random Forest and classical ensemble/model comparison.
- Keras Artificial Neural Network training.
- Early stopping and a classical-versus-neural-network comparison.

Saved results:
- The Logistic Regression baseline achieved 73.81% test accuracy.
- The selected RBF SVM achieved 74.38% test accuracy and was chosen for stronger cross-validation recall.
- Feature engineering and ensemble comparison produced a 77.36% test-accuracy model.
- The Keras ANN achieved 79.42% test accuracy.
- The stronger accuracy of the ANN was balanced against lower churn recall compared with the Day 4 model.

Business value:
The model helps identify customers who may leave so that customer-retention teams can prioritise proactive outreach. Recall is treated as important because failing to identify a departing customer can be more costly than reviewing a false positive.

Phase 2: Product and Catalog Image Classification - Days 6-8
-------------------------------------------------------------
Objective:
Recognise clothing categories from images and make model predictions explainable.

Topics completed:
- Fashion-MNIST CNN classification.
- Fashion Product Images catalog classification.
- CNN architecture design and image preprocessing.
- Training and confusion-matrix analysis.
- Transfer learning with pretrained ResNet50.
- Fine-tuning and classification of one selected catalog image.
- Grad-CAM visual explanations.

Saved results:
- The Fashion-MNIST CNN achieved 91.42% test accuracy.
- The initial catalog CNN achieved 66.88% test accuracy.
- ResNet50 transfer learning achieved 92.93% test accuracy on the catalog classification task.
- Grad-CAM reports were generated to show the image regions influencing predictions.

Business value:
The models support automated product categorisation and can help merchandising teams organise catalogs, audit labels, and understand model decisions for individual products.

Phase 3: Demand Forecasting - Days 9-10
---------------------------------------
Objective:
Forecast future sales from historical store-item demand data.

Topics completed:
- Loading and preparing store-item sales data.
- Combining duplicate observations and creating time-based sequences.
- LSTM demand forecasting.
- Next-day prediction.
- Seq2Seq sequence modelling.
- Encoder-decoder forecasting for the next seven days.
- Attention mechanisms and attention-weight visualisation.
- MAE and RMSE evaluation.
- Comparing multi-horizon forecasts with a one-day LSTM baseline.

Saved results:
- The LSTM next-day model achieved MAE 6.70 and RMSE 8.83 sales.
- The Seq2Seq attention model achieved 7-day MAE 8.72 and RMSE 11.57 sales.
- The project explicitly treats the attention model as an experiment and reports that its current one-day result did not improve on the stronger LSTM baseline.

Business value:
Forecasts can support inventory planning, purchasing decisions, replenishment schedules, and identification of periods requiring additional stock.

Phase 4: Rare-Category Vision Augmentation - Day 11
---------------------------------------------------
Objective:
Improve coverage for an underrepresented catalog category using image synthesis.

Topics completed:
- Training a convolutional Variational Autoencoder (VAE).
- Generating synthetic candidates for a rare "Free Items" category.
- Visual quality and diversity inspection.
- Optional fine-tuning of the saved Day 7 classifier.
- Comparing performance on an unchanged test split.

Current status:
- The first generated candidates were not sufficiently realistic or visually approved.
- No classifier-lift claim was made because none of the candidates was approved for retraining.
- The project demonstrates that synthetic samples require manual or automated quality control before they are used for production training.

Business value:
The VAE workflow provides a controlled way to investigate data augmentation for rare product categories without assuming that every generated image is suitable.

Phase 5: Review Sentiment and Ticket Routing - Days 12-13
----------------------------------------------------------
Objective:
Understand customer-review sentiment and demonstrate routing of review-derived support text.

Topics completed:
- NLTK text cleaning and tokenisation.
- TF-IDF feature representation.
- Logistic Regression sentiment classification.
- Identifying informative positive and negative terms.
- A classical sentiment-analysis baseline.
- Fine-tuning a pretrained DistilBERT-family model.
- Weak-label generation for ticket-routing categories.
- Sentiment and ticket-routing model comparison.

Saved results:
- TF-IDF with Logistic Regression achieved 87.33% sentiment accuracy.
- The fine-tuned BERT sentiment model achieved 91.67% accuracy on a separate experiment split.
- The generated-label ticket-routing demonstration achieved 33.65% accuracy and is not presented as production-ready.

Important limitation:
Ticket-routing labels were derived from review keywords. They demonstrate the modelling workflow, but real support-ticket routing requires a separately labelled ticket dataset and a real operational taxonomy.

Business value:
The sentiment component can help teams identify positive or negative customer feedback at scale. The routing prototype illustrates how text can be classified into operational categories, while clearly distinguishing a demonstration from a production support system.

Topics Covered Across the Project
---------------------------------
- Machine-learning preprocessing and data pipelines.
- Classical models: Logistic Regression, Decision Trees, Random Forest, SVM, and KNN.
- Cross-validation, hyperparameter tuning, learning curves, recall, accuracy, ROC-AUC, MAE, and RMSE.
- Deep learning: ANNs, CNNs, ResNet50 transfer learning, LSTMs, Seq2Seq models, attention, and VAEs.
- Explainable vision: Grad-CAM, visual overlays, and model-region interpretation.
- NLP: NLTK cleaning, TF-IDF, Logistic Regression baselines, and BERT-family fine-tuning.
- Model serialisation and reproducible reports.
- Clear separation of experimental results from claims that are not supported by the data.

Deliverables
------------
- Reusable training and prediction Python modules.
- Saved baseline, tuned, neural-network, CNN, transfer-learning, forecasting, VAE, and Transformer models.
- Jupyter notebooks documenting the day-wise implementation.
- Accuracy, recall, MAE, RMSE, confusion matrices, comparison reports, plots, forecasts, attention weights, and Grad-CAM images.

Manager Talking Points
----------------------
- RetailMind AI demonstrates both breadth and progression: it starts with classical models and advances to neural networks, transfer learning, forecasting, generative vision, and Transformers.
- The project is organised into measurable phases, and each module saves artifacts so results can be reviewed and compared.
- Retail use cases are linked to practical outcomes such as customer retention, catalog organisation, inventory planning, review analysis, and support routing.
- Evaluation is treated carefully: the project distinguishes accuracy from recall and reports when a result is a comparison, limitation, or prototype rather than a production-ready outcome.
